# A1 automatic context objectives: policy pack for approval

2026-10-27, Asia/Kuala_Lumpur. **Design and independent synthetic calculations
complete. PROPOSED; no implementation or activation approval is recorded.**

Recommend the five requested mappings as **unvalidated, normative prototype
appearance defaults**. They let an ordinary request select an appearance goal
automatically; they do not establish an externally required dress code or an
empirically validated fashion preference. Saved labels measure alignment with the
chosen goal. They cannot establish that choosing that goal was appropriate.

Recommend retaining exact duplicate-preference suppression, replacing strict
Unknown withholding with explicitly limited best-evidenced selection, and using a
disclosed, context-gated legacy baseline when no relative objective or no computable
relative candidate exists. These are decisions for approval, not changes already made.

This pack revises the earlier
[Option A proposal](CONTEXT_RELEVANCE_RANKING_DESIGN_AND_ACCEPTANCE_PROPOSAL.md).
That document and its unrun oracle retain their original bytes. In particular, its
strict missing-objective refusal, optimistic-Unknown withholding and blanket
independent-fashion-evidence prerequisite for A1 activation are **superseded proposals
only if the decisions here are approved**. Approval can authorise a labelled product
convention; it cannot substitute for independent validation or be inferred from the
user asking for this assessment.

## 2. Common applicability and authority

The catalogue operates only after the existing `general_context_v1` interpretation,
capability and mandatory eligibility checks. Its initial automatic entries require
one of the five exact semantic situations below, unspecified role, and unspecified
dress code/formality. Existing permitted optional venues/companions may be present;
omission is also permitted. Timing is metadata, not weather or future-condition proof.

The current admission registry remains `context-registry-1.4.0`, with ordinary
`appearance-2.0.0` policies. Allow/Deny, explicit code/formality and current-source
barriers retain their meanings. A soft catalogue target neither fills interpreted
`request.style` as user speech nor creates a hard formality/dress-code requirement.

|Kind of authority|What it would mean here|
|---|---|
|Normative prototype convention|The product owner explicitly chooses a default appearance goal for this bounded prototype. Its rationale and counterexamples are disclosed; independent appropriateness remains unvalidated. This is the proposed A1 basis.|
|Applicable external requirement|An actual institution/venue/event requirement, its source and applicability would need to be established. None is supplied by these broad requests. The default is not such a requirement.|
|Empirically supported preference|Independent, appropriately sampled human/context evidence would support a stated population and scope. No such evaluation was performed or supplied here.|

All five mappings are defensible **as optional-to-override product conventions**,
not deductions from event names. Date/dinner smart-casual defaults in particular
are underdetermined by those names; their justification is a chosen prototype
presentation preference, not a finding that casual is unsuitable.

## 5. Exact scoring and preference decision

Retain **T = 3/5 R + 1/5 C + 1/5 P**, with exact rational arithmetic. The proposed
relative profile is `context_relevance_decision_v1`; it does not redefine the legacy
`experimental_compatibility_decision_v1` profile or its old records.

For the one-piece, or upper and lower base members, a current admitted singleton
recorded style gives match 1 or non-match 0 against the selected target. Otherwise
it is Unknown. R is their mean when all required matches are known. Thus a matching
one-piece has R=1; one matching upper/lower member has R=1/2; two matching members
have R=1. Outerwear contributes no direct R term but must pass every hard requirement
and remains in C and applicable P. There is no one-piece midpoint in R.

Passing occasion, category, colour or formality does not add constant maximum R
terms. Known R=0 can still win if all known alternatives are worse; there is no
hidden minimum relevance cutoff. Explain that the target was not matched.

C keeps its current pairwise colour/style decision policy, Unknown-atom conventions
and one-piece reference. P keeps the four existing global positive groups and their
importance weights, with the following explicit new-profile change only. No source,
confidence, owner review or Allow earns a numeric bonus. Recorded labels that are
equivalently admitted have equal arithmetic regardless of human-review status;
their verification disclosures differ.

**Recommendation: retain exact duplicate suppression with the original denominator.**
Canonicalise each test by attribute, target set, member scope and aggregation. Only
an exactly equivalent style/base-mean preference is suppressed when already used
by R. Conflicting or different preferences remain. Pairwise style agreement in C
is a different question from absolute target alignment, though they can correlate.

```text
P_independent = sum(b_k * d_k * p_k) / sum(b_k)
d_k = 0 for an exactly duplicated R test; otherwise 1
```

Retain the original positive-weight denominator. Unknown terms supply no positive
value under the existing convention. With every active test suppressed, show
NotApplicable / duplicate-suppressed and numeric contribution 0, not NoPreferences
or missing evidence. With no active preferences, retain NoPreferences / contribution
0. Raw preferences are retained. Do not silently renormalise remaining terms.

|Exact example|Preserve original P|Recommended duplicate mask|
|---|---|---|
|Casual goal; two casual bases; C=1; lone casual-style preference|R=1,P=1,T=1|R=1,P contribution=0,T=4/5|
|Casual goal; one of two bases casual; C=1/2; same preference|R=1/2,P=1/2,T=1/2|R=1/2,P contribution=0,T=2/5|
|Casual goal; independent green preference; Green Sweatshirt + Blue Jeans|R=1,C=1/2,P=1,T=9/10|Identical: green is not the R test|
|Equal-weight casual-style and green groups; green casual pair with C=1/2|P=1,T=9/10|Style suppressed, denominator remains 2: P=1/2,T=4/5|

For the last case the non-green Blue T-shirt/Blue Jeans pair also has T=4/5, but
its C=1 beats C=1/2 at the preserved numeric tie comparison. Suppression can therefore
change a winner as well as its displayed total. This must be approved and explained.

Preserving P would be simpler to explain as the old formula, but when P is precisely
the same base-style mean as R it yields `T=4/5 R + 1/5 C`: the same semantic objective
receives both budgets. That can be an intentional product choice, not independent
support. The recommended mask avoids that duplicate payment. Renormalising P after
suppression would increase other preferences' influence and is a third policy,
excluded here. The legacy fallback always uses its original **unmasked** P.

Point candidates retain descending `(T,R,C,P)`, then ascending actual
`(role,garment_id)` key. Fractions, not rounded display decimals, decide ties.
This deterministic order says nothing about fashion superiority.

## 6. Unknown handling and usable fallback

A clean applicable Allow may admit a garment with missing style, but cannot provide
that style for R. Missing, ambiguous, stale or malformed source evidence cannot be
invented as a match. Existing mandatory malformed/Unknown barriers remain first;
relative coverage applies only to candidates that already passed those gates.

For n base members, k known matches and u unknowns:

```text
R_low = k/n; R_high = (k+u)/n
T_low/high = 3/5 R_low/high + 1/5 C + 1/5 P_independent
```

These are logical bounds on recorded-label alignment, not calibrated confidence
intervals. C's separate decision conventions remain disclosed. An upper bound is
never persisted as earned R or used to save an Unknown candidate as a relative winner.

**Recommend `best_evidenced_with_coverage_v1`:**

1. Rank point-R candidates exactly. Retain counts of mandatory Pass/Fail/Unknown,
   point-R candidates and unresolved relative candidates.
2. Compare the best point candidate with the strongest unresolved optimistic
   `(T_high,R_high,C,P,actual key)` comparison. If it wins, record a full-pool logical
   bound certificate under captured facts/conventions.
3. If an unresolved candidate could outrank it, still select the best point candidate,
   explicitly limited to candidates with computable relative evidence. Display:
   **“Best among outfits with computable style alignment. Other eligible outfits
   have missing relative evidence and could rank higher.”** Record coverage counts
   and `global_optimum_certified=false`. This changes the earlier strict withholding
   proposal; do not claim best overall contextual outfit.
4. If there is no point-R candidate but hard-Pass complete outfits exist, use the
   disclosed baseline below. If none pass, retain the current controlled no-result.

This avoids an unresolved competitor blocking every otherwise useful result, while
acknowledging bias toward evidence-complete candidates. It does not guess missing
values or ask the user to review/relabel every garment. The tradeoff is explicit:
an Unknown outfit may truly be better. Owners preferring a provable whole-pool relative
optimum can instead approve strict withholding, accepting more no-results.

The synthetic sensitivity check deletes one of eight style labels across two goals,
Allow present/absent and no-preference/dress-preference conditions: **64 deliberately
constructed cases**. Strict optimistic withholding would block six despite a known
relative candidate; this recommendation returns six coverage-limited results instead.
All six occur with Allow admitting missing facts: two without preferences and four
with the dress preference. Without Allow, affected outfits remain mandatory Unknown
and never enter ranking. These frequencies estimate **no normal-wardrobe rate**.
Actual prevalence and whether users understand the limitation require later UAT.

Retain controlled refusal for existing unsupported capability cases; malformed configuration remains an error.|
|B. For B, propose `baseline_disclosed_v1` with reasons `objective_unavailable` or
`no_point_relative_candidate`. Score the **same hard-Pass pool** with the actual
legacy profile, retaining its old R, C, P and tie semantics. Show **“Baseline fallback:
contextual appearance gates applied; differentiated contextual relevance was not
established.”** Relative R is unavailable; any old R=1 is labelled **legacy request
match**, not relative relevance. Totals across these profiles are not comparable
fashion-confidence values. No missing-objective state is disguised as a garment fact.

No fallback may ignore a meaningful request condition, admit a mandatory Fail/Unknown,
enable unsupported context/role/code, substitute a generic request, repair data, or
hide malformed policy/hash/proof or a technical failure. Missing catalogue entries
are a normal versioned state; corrupt catalogue files are not. A coverage-limited
relative result is not replaced by baseline merely because a competitor is unresolved.

## 8. Independent human evaluation plan — not performed

The proposed defaults have **no independent validation claim**. A practical small
FYP study can separate convention validity, ranking mechanics and user understanding:

1. Pre-register forty held-out comparisons, eight per context, using lawful inputs
   outside the eight demonstration garments. Include equal good choices, casual
   date/dinner counterexamples, dressed-up shopping/outings, missing facts and cases
   with no justified ordering. State scenario facts before selecting examples; avoid
   fabricated venues/codes. Keep development and held-out garments/scenarios separate.
2. If available, recruit two independent reviewers; record relevant experience and
   conflicts instead of automatically calling them fashion experts. Obtain blind
   A/B/tie/no-justified-order/insufficient-information judgements with reasons before
   showing catalogue targets or scores. Compare old/new winners with counterbalanced
   left/right presentation. Identical winners are recorded as identical, not forced
   preference evidence. Forty cases * two reviewers means eighty initial ratings,
   not eighty independent scenarios. Optional repeat ratings measure consistency and
   are reported separately.
3. Then disclose each prototype convention and ask separately about applicability,
   limitations and counterexamples. Report raw agreement, disagreement, ties,
   abstentions, context-specific results and uncertainty from the small sample.
   Do not resolve every disagreement into a fabricated ground truth or count ties
   as automatic wins. An applicable external code, if studied later, is a separate
   evidence category.
4. Propose five to eight consenting normal users, each trying five ordinary requests
   with held-out or their voluntarily supplied lawful data. Record whether they
   understand the target, provisional source, exact ranking score, duplicate mask,
   fallback and coverage limitation; collect perceived relevance, effort and reasons.
   Twenty-five to forty interactions would not establish population fashion accuracy.
   No participants have been recruited and no such observations exist here.

Owners/supervisor must approve the sampling and acceptance rubric before collecting
ratings. Suggested decision questions are whether each convention remains useful
after counterexamples, whether users can distinguish a soft goal from a code, and
whether fallback/coverage is understood without repeated garment review. No universal
accuracy percentage or retrospectively selected threshold is proposed as a fact.

If independent reviewers are unavailable, owner UAT and author-assigned prototype
labels remain the available evidence. Record independent validation OPEN; seek
reviewers for stronger claims later. Engineering conformance plus explicit normative
policy approval can support a bounded prototype release, **if this revised release
criterion is expressly approved**. It cannot support claims of validated event
appropriateness, real-model accuracy, comfort, safety or professional judgement.
